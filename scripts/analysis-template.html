INTERNATIONAL BORDERS / 2016–2026

Understanding border closures

Closures may vary in terms of where the border is closed, which side of it is closed, for whom it is closed, and how effectively the closure is enforced.

MEANING & SCOPE

What is a border closure?

The phrase “the border is closed” conjures the image of a gate, slammed shut and bolted, and sets up a binary distinction in which borders are either completely open or completely closed. This quickly falls apart on closer inspection.

If border closures are akin to locked properties, is the gate locked from the inside, the outside, or both? How does the occupant access food and critical supplies? Is there another gate?

AUTHORITY

Which side?

One border may be closed while the other is open. Alternatively, both may be closed to different degrees, to different people, at different times, and in different places.

GEOGRAPHY

Where?

A restriction may cover a national frontier, one border with a neighbour or an individual crossing. Other routes may remain available.

DIRECTION

Which way?

Entry, exit and transit can face different rules. Certain groups may be allowed to leave but not to return.

PEOPLE & GOODS

For whom?

Border closures may apply to certain groups in society and not to others. Citizenship, residence, occupation and the goods being carried can determine permission to cross.

TIME

When?

Announcement, implementation, easing and reopening are distinct moments. A restriction can change scope or pause before ending.

ENFORCEMENT

Is it enforced?

An official prohibition does not establish what happens on the ground. Observed passage, authorised exceptions and informal routes reveal how the restriction operates.

RATIONALES

Why are borders closed?

Separating out closures based on their recorded rationales has both explanatory and analytical value. The boundaries between these categories are not sealed, and there will always be exceptions.

An official rationale is the explanation stated by the authority imposing a restriction. Some of these headings also describe subjects of protest by non-state actors who block crossings. An attributed explanation records what an actor says; it does not, by itself, establish underlying intent.

BORDERWORK

How does a closure work?

Borderwork refers to the broad range of human activities associated with an international border. This includes such things as customs duties, immigration and emigration, border security and the everyday upkeep of border posts. It exists in parallel to the activities of people who interact with the border, such as traders, migrants, and smugglers.

Closure changes this work. Routine processing may diminish while enforcement and negotiation over exceptions become more prominent. Reopening also requires practical coordination; it need not restore the arrangements that existed before.

A borderlands perspective follows the relationships that cross the frontier: livelihoods, markets, families and access to services. Consequences can differ across the two sides and extend well beyond the crossing itself.

One blockade, changing scope

At Siret–Porubne, separate records describe a blockade of trucks in both directions and a later phase affecting entry into Romania. They capture a change within the same episode.

What do the records show?

Security and health and biosecurity are the most frequent labels in the collected records. The counts describe an incomplete inventory, with related policies and phases recorded separately. They do not estimate the global frequency of independent closure decisions.

How are these areas assigned?

Records originally grouped under Europe or Americas are assigned by their first listed country or territory. This is a catalogue convention, not a claim about which authority imposed the restriction. A record involving several subregions is counted once; its full country list remains available in the record.

Europe uses Northern, Western, Southern and Eastern subdivisions. Northern Europe includes the Nordic and Baltic countries and the UK and Ireland; Western Europe includes France, Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Benelux countries. Southern Europe includes the Mediterranean and western Balkan countries, including Cyprus. Eastern Europe includes Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Belarus, Moldova, Ukraine and Russia.

North America here includes Canada, the United States and Mexico. Central America covers Belize through Panama; the Caribbean is separate. South America includes French Guiana, and the Saint-Martin/Sint Maarten crossing is in the Caribbean. Existing South America records are combined with the new subdivision.

Other workbook research groups retain their original labels. The original group is shown in each record and retained as “Region” in record CSV exports, alongside the derived “Research area”.

SOURCES & METHOD

How to read this research

This register brings together reported restrictions at international land borders and cross-border river ferries between 21 October 2016 and 21 October 2026. It includes restrictions on particular travellers or goods, as well as broader closures. Disputed boundaries are identified separately.

There are {{INCLUDED}} included records. A record is an entry compiled for this dataset from one or more sources. It describes a restriction at a specified crossing, group of crossings or wider border policy, and may contain several dated phases. Related records can overlap, so their totals do not equal a count of independent closure decisions.

What is included?

Short land-border closures are included. Routine nightly opening hours, individual visa refusals and checks that still permit passage are excluded. Airports, sea-only restrictions and island-state air-entry rules are outside this register. {{EXCLUDED}} additional records are retained for reference but excluded from the charts and map: the reported Burundi entry ban (GL18), the cancelled Benin election closure (SUP-004) and the unresolved Madaniyat crossing case (R3-SC-004). Their individual records explain the evidence and inclusion decisions. SA025 contributes its documented rail suspension only; the road disruption remains excluded.

How complete is it?

The register is incomplete. Research coverage differs by place, period and source access. No record for a country does not mean its borders remained open. The charts describe the collected evidence and should not be read as global closure rates.

What do the dates mean?

A start, a last report of closure and a reopening are different facts. Where a reopening is unknown, the date of the last report does not fill that gap. Disputed dates and isolated observations stay visible without implying a continuous closure between them.

Days are calculated by subtracting dates. Same-day episodes have no whole-day duration; overlapping phases are not added together. A partial reopening applies only to the named traffic and location.

What do the lines and colours mean?

The recorded reason for a restriction is indicated by its colour; the map key identifies each category. Dots locate named crossings. Lines represent restrictions reported to apply more widely: a solid line means border-wide scope is stated, while a dotted line means the wider rule has limited or uncertain geographical extent. Read the record for the actual location and affected movement.

More than one rationale can apply. Codes record attributed explanations, not proof of every actor’s underlying motive.

How were sources reviewed?

Research, challenge and adjudication checked the source evidence for occurrence, dates, movement and geographical scope, continuity and reported reasons. Current conclusions take precedence; earlier findings remain labelled as historical. Completing a review does not resolve every attribute.

Source links distinguish full pages, search excerpts and failed access. Different websites can repeat the same report. {{QUEUE_ITEMS}} evidence questions remain open across {{QUEUE_RECORDS}} records.

How should the duration chart be used?

It compares documented phases with exact dates and an observed reopening. The companion coverage chart shows how few records may qualify in each category. Related phases and shared rationale labels prevent a causal interpretation; the plot does not establish a typical duration for all closures.
